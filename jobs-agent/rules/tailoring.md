# Tailoring rules

## The hard constraint

Every biographical or experiential claim must trace to a line in `profile/facts.md`.
Reordering, reframing and emphasis are allowed. Invention, inflation and implication are not.

Specifically forbidden:
- Claiming a gap listed under "NEVER claim these"
- Implying domain experience through adjacency ("familiar with aviation systems")
- Converting a single instance into a pattern ("extensive experience with X" from one project)
- Inventing metrics, team sizes, dates or outcomes
- Naming a company, tool or person not in the fact base

If a job requires something not in the fact base, **say so in the output** rather than working around it.

---

## Structure of a tailored application

### 1. Title line
Three elements, drawn from what the role actually centres on. Examples that have worked:
- `PRODUCT LEADER | MULTI-SIDED PLATFORMS, MARKETPLACES & B2B2C`
- `PRODUCT LEADER | AI-NATIVE PRODUCTS, EVALUATION AND OPERATING MODELS`
- `PRODUCT LEADER | DATA PRODUCTS, MONETIZATION AND B2B SAAS`

Do not use "Digital Product Leader" — too generic.

### 2. Intro paragraph — one paragraph, ~90 words
Open on the shape of the problem the role describes, not on years of experience. Then evidence. Then, if the role's own framing invites it, one line on how he leads.

The opening sentence should make the reader think *this person has done this exact shape of thing before*, even when the industry differs.

### 3. Core competencies — five or six, one line each
Ordered to match the role's stated priorities, not a fixed template. Lead with whatever the JD lists first in its requirements. Each should carry at least one concrete outcome from the fact base, not a list of nouns.

Weak: `Marketplace Products: consumer and B2B marketplaces, transactions, automotive`
Strong: `Marketplace products: a managed transaction model launched to 100,000 transactions in eight weeks, and a dealer product grown past 1,000 active customers`

### 4. The gap paragraph — mandatory when a gap exists
Name it once, plainly, early-ish, then pivot to what transfers. Never apologise, never bury it, never repeat it.

Pattern that has worked repeatedly:
> "I don't yet bring [the specific domain vocabulary]. What I bring is [the capability the role is actually built on], plus [any genuine adjacent or user-side perspective]. The domain specifics are a ramp, not a wall."

### 5. Cover note — five short paragraphs maximum
Interest → structural parallel → evidence → honest gap → why this company specifically. Close on something from their own material that genuinely matched, not flattery.

---

## Style

- Plain, direct sentences. No em-dashes.
- No "passionate," "results-driven," "proven track record," "synergy," "leverage" as a verb.
- Numbers over adjectives.
- British/international English spelling.
- If the application is in Finnish, keep the same structure; do not over-formalise.

## Self-check before output

1. Can every claim be pointed at a line in `facts.md`?
2. Is the gap named exactly once?
3. Does the first sentence describe the shape of their problem?
4. Are there five or six competencies, not nine?
5. Is the company name correct everywhere? (Naming the wrong company ends an application.)
6. Has anything been quietly upgraded from "once" to "extensive"?
